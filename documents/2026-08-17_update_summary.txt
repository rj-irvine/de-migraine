DE MIGRAINE / HEADACHE STUDY (25P01)
Update summary - 17 August 2026
Ryan Irvine (CDS) for Paula Chu (OR)


TWO TERMS

- Persistence: how long a patient stays on treatment before stopping or
  switching.
- Adherence: whether they keep taking it, measured as the share of days in a
  year they had medication in hand.


WHAT CHANGED

- Adherence corrected. Patients who started treatment late were being dropped
  instead of counted as untreated. They were 55% of the matched group against
  38% of headache patients, which made the matched group look more adherent.

- Prescription length now estimated drug by drug. Only a quarter of
  prescriptions record how long to take the medication. Missing ones used a
  flat 30 days; they now use the typical length for that same drug. The flat
  value accounted for roughly 60% of the apparent difference between groups.

- Persistence can now be reported. It was previously exactly 30 days for
  everyone, which was the filled-in value rather than a finding. It is now 13
  days in both groups, and two thirds of first courses are a single
  prescription.

- One adherence measure instead of two. The old one gave a perfect score to
  anyone with a single prescription. Removed.

- Study period corrected to January 2021, from December 2016 carried over
  from the UK study. Patient counts unaffected.

- Presentation: tables now show drug names rather than codes, and footnotes
  are rewritten in plain language.


WHAT IT MEANS

- Main findings unchanged. Headache patients receive far more painkillers,
  more of it migraine-specific, average 3.3 courses of treatment against 1.9,
  and a third work through four or more drugs against 18%. These held under
  every alternative assumption we tested.

- Adherence should not be presented as a finding. On equal terms it is 1.6%
  for headache patients and 1.9% for the matched group. That is not poor
  adherence. It is a measure built for daily medication applied to drugs taken
  only when an attack starts, which is also why migraine-specific drugs carry
  a treatment duration on just 3% of prescriptions.


FOR DISCUSSION

- Should persistence, adherence and lines of therapy be reported for the
  matched group at all? The comparison is useful, but the wording implies
  those patients are being treated for headache when they are not.


KNOWN LIMITS

- Over-the-counter painkillers are not captured, so real use is higher than
  any figure here.
- The quantity field is unusable, so total volume dispensed is not reported.
